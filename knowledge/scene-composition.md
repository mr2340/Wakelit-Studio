# Scene Composition & Cinematography (Wakelit)

> **Purpose:** Framing, shot scales, camera discipline, and stage rules.
> **Source:** `AGENT_BRIEF_01`, `AGENT_BRIEF_02`, and Day One Films reference analysis.

---

## 1. Frame Geometry & Letterbox Discipline
- **Canvas:** 1920 × 1080 (16:9).
- **Letterbox Bars:** Top bar: `y = 0 to 96`; Bottom bar: `y = 984 to 1080`.
- **Active Safe Zone:** `y = 96 to 984` (Height: 888 px).
- **Ground Line:** The floor line sits horizontally at **y ≈ 750–800** (70–75% down the frame).

---

## 2. Standard Shot Scales

### 1. Wide Shot (WS) — Context & Isolation
- **Character Scale:** Character stands at approximately **45%–50%** of total active picture height; seated at **30%–35%**.
- **Placement:** Placed on either the left or right third (x ≈ 640 or x ≈ 1280), leaving generous negative space for captions, room atmosphere, or the Shade.
- **Narrative Use:** Establishing the bedroom, the dark street, the corridor, or emphasizing loneliness.

### 2. Medium Shot (MS) — Interaction & Focus
- **Character Scale:** Character framed from waist up. Fills approximately **45%–55%** of frame height.
- **Placement:** Centered or slightly off-center.
- **Narrative Use:** Working at desk, sitting on bed edge, talking with friend at bus stop or cafe table.

### 3. Close-Up (CU) — Internal Emotion
- **Character Scale:** Head and hood fill **55%–65%** of active picture height.
- **Placement:** Centered horizontally (`x = 960`).
- **Caption Relocation Rule:** In face close-ups, bottom captions MUST move to the **top** (`y ≈ 182`) or side so text never obscures Ayo's face.
- **Narrative Use:** Critical emotional turns, exhaustion, shock, or determination.

### 4. Insert / POV Shot — Devices & Objects
- **Phone Close-Up:** Smartphone displayed vertically in center frame, held by two mustard mitten hands on left/right borders. Background room is heavily blurred.
- **Digital Clock:** Bedside red LED digits (`#D6453D`) framed sharp, displaying narrative timestamps (e.g. `23:41` → `01:07`).
- **Calendar / Notebook:** Flat overhead or slight angle on handwritten pages.

---

## 3. Camera Movement Rules
- **Locked Camera (Default):** 95%+ of shots are on a locked tripod. Characters and light move, not the camera.
- **Slow Push-In (Permitted Variation):** A very slow, barely perceptible push-in (scale from 100% to 106% over 4 seconds) on intense held moments.
- **Prohibited:** Never use handheld camera shake, Dutch tilts, rapid zooms, or whip-pans.
