## 1. Acting Philosophy: The Power of Understated Pantomime

Wakelit characters do NOT gesticulate wildly, flail their arms, or perform cartoon slapstick. The emotional core of Wakelit lies in **internalized tension expressed through micro-motion**:
- **Stillness Precedes Action:** A character frozen in indecision communicates deeper psychological weight than one moving aimlessly. Pauses and hesitation are active beats.
- **Micro-Motion Hierarchy:** When the body is still, the eye follows subtle shifts:
  1. *Gaze direction & eyelid heaviness:* Looking away from a task vs staring blankly.
  2. *Head tilt & shoulder drop:* Dropping 5–10 degrees communicates crushing exhaustion.
  3. *Breathing rhythm:* Slow, shallow chest heave in screen trance vs sharp, decisive intake upon resolution.
  4. *Hand posture:* Mitten hands resting limp on a desk vs curling into a determined fist or gripping a shoelace.
- **Locked Camera Discipline:** The camera remains static on a tripod (zero zoom, zero handheld jitter). The character's movement within the frame supplies 100% of the cinematic energy.

## 3. The Shade's Movement Dynamics

The Shade is non-biological and obeys distinct physical laws:
1. **Fluid Silhouette:** Smoky obsidian silhouette with zero sharp anatomical bone structure. Edges billow slowly like heavy underwater ink.
2. **Horizontal Pale-Blue Slit Eyes (`#9CC4FF`):**
   - Remain perfectly horizontal and level even when the body shifts.
   - Soft, hypnotic gaze. The Shade never scowls or attacks aggressively; it lures with soothing, seductive comfort.
3. **Spatial Scale Evolution:**
   - *Act I (Beats 1–3):* Small, curled behind Ayo's chair or hovering near the desk edge like a quiet pet.
   - *Act II (Beats 4–6):* Swells to twice Ayo's volume, draping over his shoulders like a weighted velvet blanket.
   - *Act III (Beats 8–11):* Confronts Ayo in the mirror; shrinks violently when rejected; dissipates into white vapor upon sun exposure.

## 4. Movement Continuity & Shot Linking Law

To ensure clean editing without visual jumps:
- **Action In, Action Out:** If Ayo begins turning his head in Shot N, Shot N+1 must cut on the apex of that turn.
- **Eye-Trace Preservation:** The audience's focal point at the cut point of Shot N must align within 15% screen radius with the primary focal point of Shot N+1.
- **The 2-Second Motion Rule:** No clip holds completely motionless for more than 2.0 seconds. A subtle chest rise, eyelid blink, or ambient steam keeps the frame organically alive.
